Appendix A — Climate change drove the deep-time assembly of biodiversity

A.0.1 Paleoatmospheric temperature data

All climate rasters were aligned to a common spatial grid so that each pixel represents the same geographic location through time. Grid cells were retained only when at least one valid observation was present, ensuring that analyses relied on empirical information rather than interpolation alone. For each grid cell, temperature values through time were treated as a time series, and a smooth trajectory was estimated using a generalized additive model (GAM). Spatially explicit reconstructions of mean annual surface temperature across the Northern Hemisphere from 23 to 2 Ma are shown at 1-Myr intervals and 1° × 1° spatial resolution. Each panel represents a single time slice and is displayed using a common color scale to allow comparison across time. These layers form the basis for estimating directional temperature change and climatic instability used in subsequent analyses.

A.0.2 Obtaining the rate of climate change

Once smoothed trajectories are obtained, temperature change is quantified as the difference between consecutive time steps. These first differences approximate the rate and direction of temperature change through time, allowing spatially explicit assessment of climatic dynamics rather than static climate conditions.

A.0.3 Estimating climate instability

Climate instability is measured as the standard deviation of mean temperature between consecutive time steps within a moving window of +-2Ma. This captures how variable the atmospheric temperature rather than the warming or the cooling trend. Lower values indicate more stable environments

A.1 Statistical analyses

A.1.0.1 Spatial binning of fossil locations into fossil paleoassemblages

Fossil occurrences were aggregated into standardized paleoassemblages using a spatiotemporal binning procedure. Records were first assigned to continents and then spatially grouped into regularly spaced bins (~100 km) to reduce the effects of uneven sampling intensity. Each spatial bin was combined with a discrete temporal bin based on rounded midpoint ages to define unique spatiotemporal assemblages. These paleoassemblages provide comparable units of community composition for subsequent analyses of turnover and paleobiogeographic structure.

A.2 Sensitivity analyses

We tested four model configurations that represent different assumptions about how smooth temperature trajectories are through time. When analyzed together, they provide a sensitivity analysis to understand the impact of different temporal smoothing choices for the shape of temporal climatic trajectories and climate-fossil turnover relationships.

A.2.1 Sensitivity of fossil paleoassemblages to spatial binning

Spatial aggregation of fossil occurrences was performed using the H3 hierarchical hexagonal indexing system (developed by Uber Engineering; Brodsky, 2018), which partitions the Earth’s surface into equal-area hexagonal cells across multiple nested resolutions.

We used four resolutions spanning a broad range of spatial grains:

  • h0 (~1918 km spacing) – continental-scale aggregation

  • h1 (~725 km) – subcontinental scale

  • h2 (~274 km) – regional scale

  • h3 (~104 km) – local scale

At each resolution, fossil occurrences were assigned to H3 cells and aggregated into paleo-assemblages, with each cell representing a sampling unit for richness and turnover analyses. Varying spatial resolution allowed us to assess how assemblage definition influences estimates of diversity and turnover, as well as the sensitivity of GDM results to spatial grain.

A.2.2 Sensitivity of Neogene temperature‐change estimates

  • gam_6_3 (df = 6, degree = 3): Allows relatively high flexibility. Temperature trajectories can follow finer temporal variation while remaining smooth, capturing long-term trends as well as moderate shorter-term fluctuations.

  • gam_4_3 (df = 4, degree = 3): An intermediate level of smoothing. With fewer degrees of freedom, the model emphasizes broader temporal patterns while still allowing nonlinear trajectories.

  • gam_4_2 (df = 4, degree = 2): A more conservative specification. Lower polynomial degree and fewer degrees of freedom produce smoother trajectories that highlight long-term climatic change while reducing sensitivity to short-term variation.

  • gam_8_3 (df = 8, degree = 3): The most flexible specification. With more degrees of freedom, the model can track finer-scale temporal changes, allowing the fitted trajectory to follow more detailed fluctuations in the temperature reconstructions.

A.2.3 Sensitivity of GDM predictive power

We evaluated the sensitivity of GDM performance (deviance explained) to spatial resolution and spline parameterization, providing a direct assessment of how modeling choices influence inference of climate–turnover relationships. The sensitivity of GDM predictive power to spatial resolution provides direct evidence that fossil turnover is governed by scale-dependent assembly processes, where climate constraints are strong but spatially localized, and become obscured under aggregation.
